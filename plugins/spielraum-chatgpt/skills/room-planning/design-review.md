# Furnish and review a home

Start with a room program, an inventory of verified fixed features, and an
assumption about occupants. Inspect the existing plan in 2D; use 3D for visual
balance, scale, alignments and what people see from the entrances. Distinguish
the actual walls and openings from room labels. Plumbing, radiators, sill
heights, appliance requirements and access needs that are missing stay unknown.

1. Reserve routes from the entrance to each room, the loggia and each required
   use position. Identify the narrowest doorway and each tight turn first.
   Compare the furnished plan with the same unfurnished geometry and settings
   when an isolated copy is available. Leave an open route through door swings
   and during normal use, not just with chairs pushed in.
2. Write the functions and relationships room by room. Compare two possible
   arrangements for any bottleneck before placing small items. Establish the
   main furniture group first, then closed storage, task lighting and textiles.
   Check room entry, use positions and both views after saving; repair genuine
   conflicts and read back the new revision before describing it as finished.
3. Assign a solid wall to each bed headboard, kitchen run and piece of wall-backed
   furniture. Default to flush placement and leave the required access at the
   sides and foot of beds. For each continuous run, name its members, order,
   wall face, reference front and target clearance before placing it. Compute
   adjoining edges from rotated extents. Measure the two ends, every seam and
   front alignment after saving, including custom models. Avoid unexplained
   slivers: use a deliberate filler or service gap only when the design warrants
   one. Respect device ventilation, hinge sides, handles and access to services.
   Never infer appliance or site installation tolerances from a proxy model.
4. Check actual use: pulled-out and occupied seats, cabinet fronts, doors and
   drawers, bed approach, and access to windows. Evaluate reasonable simultaneous
   uses where relevant (e.g. walking to the loggia with a diner seated). A clear
   room target only proves a modeled route to some area in that room.
   For a dining route that also serves another room, test a second layout state
   with all relevant chairs pulled out at once in a disposable copy. Confirm the
   connection still works. Label this a chair-position proxy; it does not include
   people, moving chairs or appliance sweeps. Use the quiet state for the saved
   furniture arrangement. If a target is missing, examine the approach around
   open door leaves and between furniture before enlarging or removing a piece.

## Get more use from the room

- Map usable stretches of wall and floor, including corners and wall space
  above furniture. Subtract door and window operation, daylight, services,
  ventilation, and the clear space each activity needs. Keep useful open areas
  for play, circulation, or flexible use.
- Match spare space to an unmet need. Compare a shallow unit, overhead storage
  or a longer work surface with the existing arrangement. Prioritize reachable
  everyday storage and continuous task space. Count a corner cupboard only
  when its front and interior can be accessed.
- In a kitchen, compare a straight run, an L and, where opposing fronts and
  circulation can fit, a U or peninsula. Locate the prep area and appliance
  approaches before filling the remaining wall. Check a full-length clear prep
  span, work aisle, corner access and the normal path past occupied dining
  seats. Use wall cabinets where they can be reached and leave the required
  headroom, task light and space for openings, extraction and service access.
  [AMK kitchen standards, choice of kitchen shape](https://www.amk.de/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/AMK-Kuechenstandards.pdf)
  and [IKEA kitchen planning guide](https://www.ikea.com/de/de/rooms/kitchen/how-to/kueche-effizient-planen-pub6d21e890/)
  illustrate these arrangements.

## Planning benchmarks by use

Use these published reference values to compare proposals. Measure between
the actual surfaces that bound the space and state which use and scenario the
measurement represents. Use the user's specified needs and known product
clearances where available. For requested accessibility, use a separate profile
for the applicable location and mobility needs, including maneuvering and
transfer areas. Record unavailable inputs as unverified.

| Use         | Reference and measurement                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Kitchen     | The [NKBA kitchen planning guidelines](https://kb.nkba.org/uploads/2022/05/Kitchen-Planning-Guidelines.pdf) recommend 91 cm for a passage and 107 cm for a work aisle for one cook or 122 cm for multiple cooks. Measure the work aisle between worktop, cabinet and appliance fronts or the opposite obstruction. Aim for a clear prep surface about 91 × 61 cm beside the sink and check appliance opening in the intended operating state.                                                            |
| Dining      | [NKBA seating guidance](https://kb.nkba.org/uploads/2022/05/Kitchen-Planning-Guidelines.pdf) recommends 81 cm from table edge to the first obstruction where nobody passes behind the diner, or 112 cm where someone regularly walks past. [IKEA Germany](https://www.ikea.com/de/de/rooms/dining/how-to/so-findest-du-den-idealen-esstisch-pxmuEeoQuGpjiwXh0U6yTPxN/) gives 110–120 cm for the latter use. In an open plan, test the actual route to the other destination in addition to the distance. |
| Living      | When the coffee table sits directly in front of the sofa, use about 41–46 cm between their facing edges as a grip and legroom target. Route primary circulation around the seat group. [Merrell et al., table 1](https://graphics.berkeley.edu/papers/Merrell-IFL-2011-08/Merrell-IFL-2011-08.pdf)                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Bedroom     | The [UK Lifetime Homes circulation guidance](https://cae.org.uk/our-services/lifetime-homes/7-circulation-space/) uses 75 cm beside and at the foot of the main double bed and on one side of a secondary bed. Review each required approach from its entry point. Count space at the foot in a secondary bedroom when that is a needed route.                                                                                                                                                           |
| Bath        | Assess approach to each fixture and check the relevant door and shower screen positions. Determine whether the actual fixture and entrance can share the clear space in ordinary use. For accessible scenarios, see the [Bayerische Architektenkammer housing guide](https://www.byak.de/data/pdfs/Beratungsstelle_Barrierefreiheit/BB_Leitfaden_2_barrierefrei.pdf).                                                                                                                                    |
| Screen work | Check direct glare and reflections using the positions of the chair, screen and window. With side daylight, the [BAuA screen work guidance](https://www.baua.de/DE/Angebote/Regelwerk/ASR/ASR-A6) recommends looking parallel to the window front.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

## Compare alternatives with the same metrics

- Hard blockers: count confirmed physical intersections and obstructed doors,
  plus any required use position with a confirmed conflict. Report unknowns
  separately. Repair confirmed blockers before ranking alternative layouts.
- Planning targets: for each relevant shortfall, give the location, use,
  measurement endpoints, measured distance and reference target. Compare fewer
  or smaller pieces when the available space misses an essential target.
- Circulation: report rooms and required use targets reachable at the same
  configured widths for bare, before and after plans. Record the narrowest
  modeled bottleneck when known. A missing raster route means unverified, not
  blocked. The default 0.9 m and optional comparison widths are design targets,
  not compliance or accessibility claims.
  Use the bare-floor result as a ceiling for this configuration. If an area is
  already unverified when bare, report that baseline limitation separately from
  a regression introduced by furniture. Track a frequently used destination in
  the normal state and the relevant simultaneous-use scenario.
- Use coverage: mark required seats, storage fronts, bed sides, wet-room and
  appliance positions clear, conflicting or unverified. Report the numerator
  and denominator and the modeled scenario. Do not count optional unused sides
  against the design or infer a clear seated/door sweep from a static footprint.
- Fit and alignment: measure gaps and front offsets in millimeters for intended
  joins, not for every close object. Report unexplained joins versus total
  intended joins. Note bounds versus visible finish and documented service gaps.
- Use of spare space: compare the largest uninterrupted work surface, reachable
  storage added, and any useful open area gained or lost. Distinguish usable
  cabinet frontage from the outer length of the run, especially at corners.
- Design review: describe room function, daylight and lighting layers, furniture
  grouping and focal point, storage, and intentional visual continuity. Note
  which judgments were checked in 2D/3D and which need occupant/site evidence.
  Keep qualitative judgments separate from geometry; avoid one total score.
